Creutzfeldt-Jakob Disease

Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease (CJD) is a rare, degenerative, and fatal brain disorder. It is characterized by rapid mental deterioration, usually within a few months. Early symptoms include memory loss, behavioral changes, and coordination problems.

Summary

Cruetzfeldt-Jakobs primarily affects the nervous system. The nervous system is composed of your brain, spinal cord, and nerves. Using electric signals, which work as messages, they communicate across the body. Functions of a properly functioning nervous system are blushing, sweating, and blinking, while also helping to regulate thoughts, memory, learning, emotions, balance, senses, wound healing, sleep, heartbeat and breathing patterns, stress response, digestion and other body processes like puberty and aging. There are 3 different types of neurons: motor neurons, sensory neurons, and interneurons. Motor neurons take signals from the brain and spinal cord to your muscles, helping you move. Sensory neurons take information from your senses to your brain (think taste, smell, hearing, touch and sight). Interneurons communicate between the former two types and regulate movement in response to sensory information. The nervous system can be grouped into two groups: central nervous system (CNS) and peripheral nervous system (PNS). The CNS is made up of the brain and spinal cord, with the brain processing information. The PNS is made of a network of nerves, which work together to relay information to and from the brain.
