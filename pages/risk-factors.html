CJD Risk Factors

Risk factors for CJD include age, with most cases occurring in people between 60 and 70 years old, and certain genetic mutations. Other factors may include exposure to contaminated medical instruments or tissue from infected individuals.

Gender

Females are more likely to be diagnosed with Cruetzfeldt-Jakobs (CJD) than males, although the margin is small. In a study, there were 2873 male cases and 3009 female cases, or in other words, 51.2% female and 48.8% male.

Age

It differs per type. Sporadic affects people in their 60s, Familial/Inherited affects people in their 40s and 50s, and variant affects people in their late teens to thirties.

Ethnicity

It mostly stays at the normal infection rate, no matter the ethnicity, with a few exceptions, which include: Libyan Jewish immigrants to Israel, people in rural Czechoslovakia, and North African immigrants to France.

Region

It is more common in the US, China, Japan, and parts of Europe. This is mainly because of the more advanced medicine/medical surveillance and higher population. However, there is an exception for variant CJD due to the bovine spongiform encephalopathy (mad cow disease) outbreak in Britain during the 1980s and 1990s, which was about 76% of cases globally for vCJD.
