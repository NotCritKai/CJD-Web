CJD Cause & Effect

Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease (CJD) is caused by abnormal prion proteins that lead to brain damage.

Cause

Cruetzfeldt-Jakobs (CJD) is a disease caused by abnormal prions in the brain. There are four types of CJD, being Sporadic CJD, Variant CJD, Iatrogenic CJD, and Inherited/Familial CJD. There is currently no cure and all treatments are aimed to make the infected person as comfortable as possible. After symptoms begin, infected people have a median time of 4-5 months before they pass. During this time, Prions are clumping together and building up in brain tissue. This can lead to nerve cell loss and other brain damage.

Effect

Cruetzfeldt-Jakobs primarily affects the nervous system. The nervous system is composed of your brain, spinal cord, and nerves. Using electric signals, which work as messages, they communicate across the body. Functions of a properly functioning nervous system are blushing, sweating, and blinking, while also helping to regulate thoughts, memory, learning, emotions, balance, senses, wound healing, sleep, heartbeat and breathing patterns, stress response, digestion and other body processes like puberty and aging. When CJD affects the nervous system, it can lead to rapid mental deterioration, memory loss, behavioral changes, and coordination problems.
